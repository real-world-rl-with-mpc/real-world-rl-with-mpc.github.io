Real-World Reinforcement Learning with MPC Scaffolding for Dexterous Manipulation

Anonymous submission
Paper Video

Sampling-based MPC provides prior experience and temporary guidance, so a 16-DoF Allegro hand learns continuous in-hand rotation directly on hardware in 7 minutes of online RL with about 3 object drops, then completes 1000 consecutive rotations without a drop.

Abstract

Real-world reinforcement learning (RL) offers a promising route to dexterous manipulation policies that can adapt directly from physical interaction, but learning is hindered by inefficient early exploration and costly failures. We propose a framework that uses sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) as scaffolding for real-world dexterous RL, providing structured prior experience and task-directed guidance during learning without human demonstrations or corrective actions.

A small set of MPC trajectories is first used to populate an offline replay buffer and to pretrain the actor and critic. During online learning, MPC intermittently guides data collection while an off-policy Soft Actor-Critic learner trains from both prior MPC experience and newly collected physical interaction, with control gradually transitioning to the learned policy.

On continuous in-hand rotation with a 16-DoF Allegro hand, the method reaches 100% success in policy-only evaluation (5/5 trials) after 7 minutes of online RL, following initialization with 20 MPC trajectories collected on hardware in 12 minutes. Online training incurs about three object drops on average. After 20 minutes of online learning, the policy achieves more than five times the rotation speed of the MPC controller. It completes 1000 consecutive rotations over more than 110 minutes without a drop. Ablations show complementary benefits from MPC-based pretraining, retained MPC experience, and online MPC guidance. We further demonstrate rapid adaptation to different object geometries and successful goal-conditioned reorientation, showing that the framework enables efficient, low-intervention, real-world dexterous RL.

Method

Our framework uses sampling-based MPC as a scaffold for an off-policy learner, with the learned policy progressively replacing MPC during real-world interaction. Training consists of three stages. Both MPC and the learned policy produce commands through the same joint-level hand-control interface, so the resulting transitions can be used by the same off-policy learner regardless of which controller generated them. The MPC controller is a fixed guidance module: it maintains a MuJoCo digital twin synchronized with the measured robot and object state and optimizes short-horizon control trajectories with the cross-entropy method over low-dimensional motion primitives and joint-level residuals.

Overview of the framework. Stage 1: offline MPC data collection. Stage 2: pretraining of the actor (behavior cloning with standard-deviation initialization) and critic (Soft-SARSA). Stage 3: online SAC with mixed replay and MPC-policy scheduling.

Offline MPC Data and Pretraining

Before online learning, we collect a small dataset of trajectories with the MPC guidance controller. All trajectories are retained as a fixed offline replay buffer and are also used to pretrain the SAC actor and critic. Transitions are labeled with the same RL reward used online rather than the MPC objective.

Actor pretraining. Behavior cloning on the pre-tanh mean with a constant base standard deviation, return-weighted episode sampling to favour high-quality demonstrations, and epsilon clipping of target actions to avoid saturation.

Critic pretraining. A Soft-SARSA target that augments the SARSA Bellman target with an entropy bonus under the frozen BC actor, using the minimum over an ensemble of 10 target critics.

Online SAC with Mixed Replay and MPC-Policy Scheduling

Mixed replay. Each mini-batch draws 30% of its samples from the fixed MPC dataset and 70% from newly collected online experience. Transitions from MPC-guided and policy-controlled execution share one online buffer and are treated identically by the learner.

MPC-policy scheduling. The probability of executing the learned policy increases linearly from 0.5 to 1 over the first 6000 environment steps (about 10 minutes), after which MPC guidance is no longer used. The controller is sampled once every 40 steps and used for the whole chunk, which preserves temporally coherent finger motions. MPC and policy actions are never blended.

Real-World Results

Experimental setup

Experiments use a 16-DoF Allegro hand mounted on a Franka FR3 arm. The policy commands joint position targets at 10 Hz. Object pose is measured by OptiTrack motion capture and provided to both the policy observation and the synchronized MuJoCo model used by MPC. The primary task is continuous in-hand rotation of a 45 mm across-flats hexagonal object. The reward uses only rotation progress, a time penalty, a success bonus for each full rotation, and a terminal drop penalty. Before online RL, 20 MPC trajectories (about 12 minutes of interaction) initialize the replay buffer and pretraining.

Left: continuous in-hand rotation of a hexagonal object. Right: goal-conditioned reorientation toward a commanded object heading.

Ablations: the three roles of MPC are complementary

We isolate MPC replay (R), actor-critic pretraining (P), and online MPC guidance (G) across six configurations, measuring policy-only success rate, rotation speed, and cumulative object drops over 45 minutes of online learning.

  • Full method (R+P+G) reaches its first 5/5 evaluation after 7.4 minutes in all three runs and incurs about three drops on average (1, 3, and 6).
  • Pretraining cuts the time to 5/5 from about 16 to 7 minutes and drops from about 17 to 3 (Full method vs. No Pretrain).
  • Guidance alone enables learning: RL Only never completes a rotation in 45 minutes and accumulates 181 drops, while Guidance Only reaches 5/5 after 18.0 ± 2.4 minutes with 35 drops.
  • MPC replay roughly halves drops (17 vs. 35) at a comparable learning time, and reduces drops from 181 to 98 even without guidance, without by itself enabling reliable rotation.
  • Online guidance makes early learning consistent: No Guidance (R+P) needs 25.8 ± 9.4 minutes and 18 drops despite identical replay and pretraining.

Ablation results for continuous in-hand rotation: success rate, rotation speed, and cumulative drops versus wall-clock time. Shaded regions show ± one standard deviation over three runs.

Outperforms the planner that guided it

After 20 minutes of online learning, we compare the policy against direct MPC execution and a PPO policy trained in MuJoCo with domain randomization and deployed without adaptation. Over 20 successful rotations each, the sim-to-real PPO policy completes 20 of 28 trials at 31.5 ± 7.8°/s, direct MPC completes all 20 at 9.9 ± 2.8°/s, and our policy completes all 20 at 55.3 ± 2.4°/s, more than five times the speed of the planner that guided its training.

Executed continuously, the policy completes 1000 consecutive rotations without a single drop, about 110 minutes of uninterrupted operation at a mean speed of 55.2°/s.

(a) Rotation speed for sim-to-real PPO, direct MPC, and our policy. (b) Rotation speed over 1000 consecutive rotations.

Adaptation to new object geometries

Starting from the policy trained on the 45 mm hexagonal object, we adapt separately to a 35 mm hexagonal object and a 55 mm circular object. Only the geometry in the MPC model is updated: no new offline MPC data is collected, pretraining is not repeated, and the original replay buffer is not reused. Online MPC guidance alone provides task-directed exploration on the new geometry.

The transferred policy already succeeds (80% and 100% at the first evaluation), and adaptation makes it faster without sacrificing reliability: mean speed rises from 45.5 to 83.6°/s on the 55 mm circular object and from 51.0 to 74.1°/s on the 35 mm hexagonal object, with only 3 and 8 drops during adaptation. Both adapted policies complete 100 consecutive rotations without a drop.

Real-world adaptation to new object geometries, and evaluation before and after adaptation.

Goal-conditioned reorientation

The same framework is applied to goal-conditioned reorientation about an axis perpendicular to the object's principal axis, with the desired heading varied within ±20°. The policy is additionally conditioned on the goal heading, and the progress reward measures the reduction in absolute heading error. About 7 minutes of MPC interaction initialize replay and pretraining.

Mean final reaching error drops from about 17° at the first evaluation to a few degrees thereafter, with 20 drops over 45 minutes of online training. The final policy succeeds in 100 of 103 trials (97.1%) with no drops and a mean reaching speed of 20.5 ± 8.2°/s. The three failures are timeouts.
